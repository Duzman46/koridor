## Full description

Koridor is a modern board strategy game where every move creates a new decision.

Guide your pawn to your rival's starting edge while using walls wisely. On each turn, move forward or place a wall to lengthen your rival's route—but every player must always retain a path to the goal.

FEATURES

- Quick match against a rival close to your rating, or open a room and share the code
- Ranked matches with a rating, an all-time board and a weekly board
- Friends: requests, invitations to a game, and a rematch after the final move
- Preset phrases and emoji during a match — there is no free-text chat
- Report or block another player from their profile or from the room list
- Two players on one device
- Four AI opponents: Easy, Medium, Hard and Expert — Expert searches several moves ahead
- Smart online orientation that keeps each player at the bottom
- A clear turn beacon, and a warning before you leave an online match — leaving forfeits it
- Dynamic 9×9 board
- Official straight-jump and diagonal-jump mechanics
- Ten languages: English, Turkish, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), German, French, Russian, Arabic, Indonesian and Hindi
- Light and dark themes, following the system by default
- Phone and tablet support
- Sounds, haptics, and fluid animations
- Move history, restart, and undo
- Game statistics on the device and on your public profile

Playing online needs an account — an e-mail address or Google sign-in — and a username other players will see. Everything offline works without one.

The free version may show a banner on the main menu and on the play screen, and a full-screen ad when a match ends or you leave one. A one-time “Remove ads” purchase disables them permanently. No ad is ever shown during a match.

Offline modes work without an internet connection. Online play, ads, and purchase verification require internet access. See the privacy policy on the store listing for details.

## On the name

The game this belongs to the family of is **Quoridor**, a trademark registered by Gigamic S.A.
in 1997 for board games. That name is not available and must not appear in the title, the
description, or the keywords — not even as "like Quoridor".

**Koridor** is deliberately a different word, and it stays that word in every localised listing.
Two reasons, and the second is the practical one:

- A different name per language is a different app to anybody who hears about it by one of them.
- The obvious English alternative, *Barricade*, is already the title of two Quoridor apps on
  Play. Taking it would put this listing third in a search for a name it does not own.

What English needs is not a different name but the sentence the Turkish word already says by
itself, which is what the subtitle is for.
